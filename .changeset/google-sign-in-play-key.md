Sign in with Google to issue the Play service account key. `build:android --upload play-internal` that finds no `play-service-account` in the config's `secrets` adapter opens Google's sign-in page in a terminal, checks that the account administers the Play Console developer account and that Google Play has the app, then creates the service account `hot-updater-play` in a Google Cloud project you pick or a new one, gives it release access to the app in Play Console, creates its JSON key, stores it through the adapter, or in this machine's key store (`~/.hot-updater/keys/<project-key>/`) without one, and continues; `hot-updater google-play login [name]`, the shortcut for `hot-updater secrets issue play-service-account [name]`, does the same ahead of time, and ends naming the service account whose key it stored and where: Hot Updater keeps no Google session. It keeps a service account the adapter, the key store, or the environment already provides, printing where it is and its email. Keys are per machine: on one without a key, it says before signing in that it will create a new key, and how to share one key across machines instead; after creating it, it names the service account's keys page in Google Cloud, where you delete the keys no machine uses anymore. `--replace` issues a new key, stores it, and names the old key with where to delete it under the service account's keys in Google Cloud, never deleting it itself. `hot-updater secrets put` only imports a key file you have (`--service-account`). Builds without a terminal use the stored key, and refuse with `npx hot-updater google-play login` when there is none. Only the key is kept: the sign-in's access token is revoked, and a key that surely was not stored is deleted again. Until Hot Updater ships its own OAuth client, the sign-in needs a "Desktop app" OAuth client of yours in `HOT_UPDATER_GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID` and `HOT_UPDATER_GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_SECRET`, and stops before opening the browser without one.
