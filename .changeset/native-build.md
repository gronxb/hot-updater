`hot-updater native-build ios` and `hot-updater native-build android` build native apps, without `EXPERIMENTAL`: `native-build <platform> [-s <scheme>] [-o <path>] [--submit testflight|play-internal] [--dry-run] [--json]`, beside `native-build find`, `native-build delete`, and `native-build import`. They replace the experimental `build:ios` and `build:android`; `run:ios` and `run:android` are removed, with no alias: install the artifact (`xcrun simctl install`, `adb install`) or use your framework's run command. Without `--scheme`, the build asks for the scheme at a terminal, and in CI or without a terminal stops with the schemes to pass. A build returns and prints the artifact (`.apk`, `.aab`, `.ipa`, or simulator `.app`) with the values compiled into it: the MIN_BUNDLE_ID, the fingerprint hash or app version, and the default channel (the app version and channel are read from the built artifact: the app's Info.plist on iOS, and with the SDK's `aapt2` the merged manifest of the `.apk` or `.aab` on Android). The build adapter's `prebuild`, such as `expo prebuild`, now runs before the commands read the native projects. The CLI issues each MIN_BUNDLE_ID with a platform byte and random bits after the build's millisecond, so an iOS and an Android build never share one, and two builds of one platform get different ones even in the same millisecond.

`hot-updater native-build import <file> [-c <channel>] [--dry-run] [--json]` tracks an `.ipa`, `.aab`, `.apk`, or simulator `.app` (the directory or its `.app.zip`) built elsewhere, such as by EAS, Xcode Cloud, Fastlane, or an earlier release, as `native-build ios|android` tracks its own builds, with the build number it reports as the row's `build_number`, so OTAs deployed after it patch from its built-in bundle. It reads everything from the artifact: the platform, app version, build number, and app ID from the manifest or Info.plist, the channel the app reports (`production` without one), the MIN_BUNDLE_ID and the fingerprint from the string literals of the app's code (the dex files' string pool, or the Mach-O `__cstring` section of the executable or of HotUpdater's dynamic framework), and the built-in bundle. It prints what it read, then refuses an artifact without a built-in bundle (a Debug build), one built without `@hot-updater/react-native`'s build step (no MIN_BUNDLE_ID) or with several candidates, one without a fingerprint under the fingerprint strategy or without a comparable app version under the appVersion strategy, a channel given as a resource reference (unless `--channel` names the channel the app reports, as when its code sets one or it switches at runtime), and an artifact whose MIN_BUNDLE_ID is registered with another built-in bundle or scope, naming that native build. Importing the same build again succeeds and changes nothing, reporting `alreadyImported: true` under `--json`, so CI jobs can rerun. An app ID no `nativeBuild` scheme's `bundleIdentifier` or `applicationId` (by default its `packageName`) names is a warning. It then uploads the artifact and the built-in bundle and registers them with no build key and no commit; `--dry-run` reads and checks, and records nothing. The reader treats every artifact as untrusted input: it reads only the entries it needs through the zip's central directory, caps the directory, each entry it reads, and all it inflates, stops an entry that inflates past its declared size (a zip bomb), matches no absolute or `..` entry name and refuses a symbolic link or a doubled name among those it reads, writes nothing to disk by entry name, gives up after two minutes, and refuses a truncated or malformed archive, plist, or manifest as unreadable instead of crashing. `@hot-updater/protocol/native-artifact`, a subpath no app imports, exports the reader (`readNativeArtifact` over a `blobSource`, with no dependencies) and `checkNativeArtifact`.

A `nativeBuild` scheme signs with a credential the CLI resolves while it builds: `signing: { keystore }` on Android passes an `android-keystore` to Gradle as the injected signing properties of release variants, and `signing: { ascApiKey }` on iOS signs device builds with automatic signing through an `asc-api-key`. Debug variants and simulator builds resolve nothing. With `signing.ascApiKey` and no `exportOptionsPlist`, an iOS device build exports for App Store Connect, and `native-build ios` refuses a device scheme with neither before it builds. The helpers' `minBundleId` and `credentials` are optional: without them, `buildAndroid` and `buildIos` issue a platform-tagged MIN_BUNDLE_ID (`createMinBundleId` from `@hot-updater/cli-tools`) and resolve signing credentials from the environment. `@hot-updater/android-helper` and `@hot-updater/apple-helper` no longer export `runAndroid`, `runIos`, or their device types. The build log in `.hot-updater/log` keeps the whole output of `xcodebuild` (also with `xcbeautify` installed, which now only formats the progress) or Gradle, through its last line. A failed build prints why on stderr, in `--json` mode too: `xcodebuild`'s `error:` lines and failed commands, or Gradle's `What went wrong` block, else the output's last 40 lines, then `Build log: <path>`, instead of the raw start of the tool's output; the helpers throw a `BuildFailedError` (`@hot-updater/cli-tools`) with the log's path and those lines.
